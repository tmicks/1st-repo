# Lesson Content Template + Worked Examples

`docs/02-kajabi-curriculum.md` lists every lesson's title and objective, but not full lesson body copy — writing all ~70 lessons in full is a content-production project in its own right, not a one-pass deliverable. This doc gives the exact template plus two fully worked examples so a writer/VA can replicate the pattern consistently across the rest.

## The template (use for every lesson)

```
LESSON: [Name]
MODULE: [Module name]  |  PRODUCT: [Tier/Product name]
OBJECTIVE: [one sentence — what the participant can do after this lesson]

[VIDEO/TEXT BODY — 300-600 words or a 4-7 min video script]
- Hook: why this matters to *them*, right now (1-2 sentences)
- Core content: the actual teaching (3-5 short sections)
- Tie-back: how this feeds their Action Plan / next milestone

DOWNLOADS (all 6, every lesson):
- PDF summary (1-page recap of the core content)
- Worksheet (fillable, ties to this lesson's specific task)
- Checklist (the steps to actually do the thing)
- Template (a reusable document/format, if applicable)
- Worked example (a filled-in sample of the worksheet/template)
- Action Plan (3-5 next steps, feeds the participant's overall Financial/Housing/Business plan)
```

## Worked example 1 — Orientation product

**LESSON: Welcome**
MODULE: (top-level, no module) | PRODUCT: Economic Stability Service (Orientation)
OBJECTIVE: Participant understands what the Institute is, how the three tracks connect, and what to expect.

> Welcome. If you're here, it's because you're ready to remove something that's been in your way — a credit barrier, a housing barrier, or the gap between where your business is and where it needs to be to grow.
>
> The Economic Stability Service is built around three tracks: Financial Stability, Housing Access, and the Entrepreneur Pathway. Most people start in Financial Stability, because a stable financial foundation is what makes housing and business goals possible — but your coach will confirm the right starting point for you specifically after your Beginning Assessment.
>
> Every step you take here is tracked — every lesson, every milestone, every certificate — so you always know exactly where you stand and what's next. You're not doing this alone: you'll have a coach, a community of people on the same path, and a weekly live call.
>
> Next: complete Orientation, then your Beginning Assessment. That's what unlocks Tier 1.

DOWNLOADS: PDF (program one-pager) · Worksheet (n/a for this lesson — use a "What I want out of this program" reflection worksheet instead) · Checklist (Getting Started checklist: log in, join community, add first call to calendar) · Template (n/a) · Example (n/a) · Action Plan (Your First 7 Days: complete Orientation, complete Beginning Assessment, join your track's community group, add Weekly Live Call to calendar).

## Worked example 2 — Tier 1, Module 1

**LESSON: Personal Financial Snapshot**
MODULE: Module 1 — Financial Assessment | PRODUCT: Tier 1: Financial Stability, Credit & Banking
OBJECTIVE: Participant documents a complete, honest picture of current income, debt, savings, and obligations as the baseline for everything that follows.

> You can't build a plan from a guess. Before we touch a budget, a credit report, or a banking pattern, we need one honest snapshot of where things stand today — no judgment, just facts.
>
> A Personal Financial Snapshot has four parts: what's coming in (all income sources, even irregular ones), what's going out (every recurring obligation, not just the big ones), what you have saved (even $40 counts), and what you owe (every debt, every account, listed — this is not the moment to leave anything off because it's uncomfortable).
>
> Most people underestimate their expenses and overestimate their savings buffer. That's normal — it's exactly why this exercise exists. Once this snapshot is done, your coach will use it to help you build your first real budget in the next lesson.
>
> This snapshot becomes the baseline your coach compares against at your Midpoint Assessment, so accuracy now matters more than looking good now.

DOWNLOADS: PDF (why a snapshot matters + how bureaus/lenders use similar data) · Worksheet (fillable 4-section snapshot form: Income / Expenses / Savings / Debts) · Checklist (documents to gather first: pay stubs, bank statements, debt statements) · Template (blank snapshot template, reusable each quarter) · Worked example (a filled-in sample snapshot for a fictional participant) · Action Plan (Complete your snapshot within 3 days; bring it to your first coaching call).

## How to scale this to the remaining ~68 lessons

1. Assign one writer (or use this as a prompt pattern with an AI drafting tool) per Tier, working module by module in the order listed in `02-kajabi-curriculum.md`.
2. For every lesson, fill in the template's four sections — Hook, Core content, Tie-back, Downloads — using the lesson's stated objective as the anchor.
3. Have the relevant coach (Financial/Housing/Business) review each module's lessons before publishing — they'll catch anything that doesn't match how the program actually runs day-to-day.
4. Launch does not require every download to be a polished final PDF — Phase 7 of `docs/12-kajabi-build-runbook.md` explicitly treats content-quality upgrades as an ongoing backlog, not a launch blocker. A plain worksheet beats a missing one.
