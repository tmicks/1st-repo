## Module 1 — Financial Assessment

### Budget Builder
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant converts their Financial Snapshot into a working monthly budget.
**Key teaching points:**
- Start from actual numbers in the Snapshot, not aspirational ones — a budget built on wishful thinking fails in week one.
- Fixed vs. variable: separate what can't change month to month (rent, loan payments) from what can (groceries, entertainment).
- The 3-bucket method: needs, obligations, discretionary — assign every dollar of income to one bucket before spending it.
- Build in a buffer line item, even $20/month — a budget with zero slack breaks at the first surprise expense.
- This budget is a draft — it gets revised again in Module 4's Financial Stability Plan once credit and banking work is done.
**Practical application:** Fillable budget worksheet using the 3-bucket method, populated from Snapshot data.
**Downloads:** standard 6-item set; Template = reusable monthly budget sheet; Example = filled-in sample budget.

### Income Analysis
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant identifies every income source, its stability, and any gaps.
**Key teaching points:**
- List every source: primary job, side income, benefits, child support, gig work — lenders and landlords will ask about all of it later, so get comfortable listing it now.
- Stability rating per source: guaranteed, likely, or variable — this matters more for a budget than the raw total.
- Identify timing: weekly, biweekly, monthly — mismatched timing between income and bills is a common hidden cause of shortfalls.
- Flag any gap between current income and the income needed to hit their stated goal (from the Beginning Assessment) — this becomes a coaching conversation, not something to solve in this lesson.
**Practical application:** Income inventory worksheet with stability and timing columns.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Expense Analysis
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant categorizes all expenses and identifies real reduction targets.
**Key teaching points:**
- Pull 60-90 days of actual transactions, not memory — memory undercounts spending by 20-30% on average.
- Categorize into fixed, variable-necessary, and variable-discretionary.
- Look for "leak" categories: subscriptions, fees, small recurring charges that add up without being noticed.
- Pick 1-2 realistic reduction targets, not five aggressive ones — the goal is a plan they'll actually follow.
**Practical application:** Expense categorization worksheet + a "leak finder" checklist.
**Downloads:** standard set.

## Module 2 — Credit Support

### Understanding Credit
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands what a credit score is and why it matters for their specific goals (housing, business).
**Key teaching points:**
- A credit score predicts the likelihood of repaying debt as agreed — it's not a moral judgment, it's a number lenders and landlords use to manage risk.
- Three major bureaus (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion) each hold slightly different data, so scores can differ across them.
- Score ranges and what they typically unlock: which range affects mortgage rates, which affects rental approval, which affects business lending terms.
- Credit is a tool for the Housing and Entrepreneur tracks specifically — this module exists because Tier 2 and Tier 3 both depend on it.
**Practical application:** None yet — this is foundational, worksheet begins next lesson.
**Downloads:** standard set (PDF/checklist heavy, worksheet light for this one).

### Credit Factors
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant can name the 5 scoring factors and their relative weight.
**Key teaching points:**
- Payment history (~35%): the single biggest factor — on-time payments matter more than anything else.
- Amounts owed/utilization (~30%): covered in depth in its own lesson later this module.
- Length of credit history (~15%): why closing old accounts can hurt more than help.
- Credit mix (~10%): having both revolving (cards) and installment (loans) helps, but isn't worth opening new debt just to diversify.
- New credit/inquiries (~10%): hard inquiries have a small, temporary impact — rate-shopping within a short window counts as one inquiry for major loan types.
**Practical application:** "Rank these 5 factors by your own current risk" worksheet — self-diagnostic before the report review.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Reviewing Credit Reports
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant pulls and reads all 3 bureau reports.
**Key teaching points:**
- Where to pull free reports (annualcreditreport.com or your program's designated tool) — bureau-direct paid monitoring is optional, not required for this program.
- What's on a report vs. what's not: accounts, payment history, inquiries, public records — but not the score itself (that's a separate pull).
- How to spot an error: wrong balance, an account that isn't theirs, a payment marked late that wasn't.
- What to bring to the next lesson: a printed or saved copy of all 3 reports, annotated with anything that looks wrong.
**Practical application:** Report-reading checklist used while going through all 3 bureau reports side by side.
**Downloads:** standard set; Template = a report-annotation worksheet.

### Understanding Derogatory Accounts
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands what collections, charge-offs, and judgments mean and what options exist.
**Key teaching points:**
- Definitions in plain language: collection (unpaid debt sold/assigned to a collector), charge-off (creditor writes it off as a loss, but the debt often still exists), judgment (a court has ordered payment).
- These stay on a report for a set period (generally 7 years for most derogatory marks) regardless of whether they're paid — paying doesn't erase the history, but it can change status shown.
- Options: dispute if inaccurate, negotiate a pay-for-delete or settlement (results vary and aren't guaranteed), or let it age off if disputing/negotiating isn't viable.
- This is where Credit Repair Cloud casework (staff-side, not participant-facing) picks up if a formal dispute is warranted — the participant's job here is identification, not the dispute process itself.
**Practical application:** Worksheet listing every derogatory item found in the prior lesson's report review, categorized by type.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Understanding Utilization
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant can calculate utilization and identify a target ratio.
**Key teaching points:**
- Utilization = balance ÷ credit limit, calculated both per-card and in aggregate across all revolving accounts.
- Commonly cited target: under 30% is "acceptable," under 10% is "strong" — the lower the better, not a cliff at exactly 30%.
- Two levers to move it: pay down balances, or (carefully) don't close old accounts with available limit, since closing reduces total available credit and can raise utilization even with no new spending.
- Utilization is recalculated monthly by the bureaus based on statement dates — a payment made right before the statement closes (not just the due date) can improve reported utilization faster.
**Practical application:** Utilization calculator worksheet — participant computes current per-card and aggregate ratios.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Credit Action Plan
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant builds a personal 90-day credit action plan from everything covered in this module.
**Key teaching points:**
- Pulls directly from the prior 5 lessons: which derogatory items to address (and how), current utilization vs. target, and any quick wins identified.
- Prioritization matters more than volume — pick the 2-3 actions with the biggest likely impact in 90 days, not a 15-item list that stalls.
- This plan is reviewed by the coach and becomes the tracked artifact for credit-related milestones in the participant's case record.
- If disputes are warranted, this is the hand-off point to staff-side Credit Repair Cloud casework — participant continues Module 3 in parallel, not blocked waiting on dispute outcomes.
**Practical application:** 90-day action plan template, coach-reviewed before Module 3 unlocks (module gate).
**Downloads:** standard set; this lesson's Action Plan output *is* the module's deliverable.

## Module 3 — Banking Alignment

### Why Banking Matters
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands banking relationships as a prerequisite for housing and business lending.
**Key teaching points:**
- Landlords and lenders both look at banking history as evidence of financial behavior that credit reports don't fully capture — consistency, not just balance size.
- A documented banking relationship is often required paperwork for Tier 2 (income verification) and Tier 3 (business banking) — this module is preparing for both, not just Tier 1.
- Being unbanked or underbanked (relying primarily on check-cashing, prepaid cards) actively works against approval odds later — this is addressed without judgment, as a practical fix, not a character issue.
**Practical application:** None — conceptual framing lesson.
**Downloads:** standard set, light on worksheet.

### Deposit Consistency
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant establishes a regular deposit pattern.
**Key teaching points:**
- Lenders and landlords read deposit consistency as a proxy for income stability — irregular, unpredictable deposits raise questions even when total income is adequate.
- For irregular/gig income: batch deposits on a fixed schedule (e.g., weekly) rather than depositing sporadically as cash comes in.
- Direct deposit setup where possible is the single highest-leverage action in this lesson.
**Practical application:** Deposit-schedule worksheet, especially for variable-income participants.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Cash Flow Management
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant manages timing between income and obligations to avoid shortfalls.
**Key teaching points:**
- Map income dates against bill due dates on a calendar — most cash-flow "shortfalls" are timing problems, not income problems.
- Techniques: negotiate due-date shifts with creditors, build a 1-pay-period buffer, use the Financial Calendar (Module 4) to visualize the whole month at once.
- This directly reduces overdraft/late-fee risk, which itself damages the banking relationship being built in this module.
**Practical application:** Cash flow timing worksheet (income dates vs. bill due dates on one calendar view).
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Documentation
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands what banking documentation will be needed in Tiers 2 and 3.
**Key teaching points:**
- Tier 2 (Housing): bank statements for income verification, typically 2-3 months.
- Tier 3 (Business): separate business account statements, distinct from personal — commingling personal/business funds is flagged here as something to avoid going forward.
- Where to access statements (online banking export/download) and how to organize them for quick retrieval later — saves real time at the Tier 2/3 document-request stage.
**Practical application:** Document-readiness checklist: what to save, where, starting now.
**Downloads:** standard set.

## Module 4 — Financial Stability Plan

### Budget
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant finalizes their working budget using everything learned in Modules 1-3.
**Key teaching points:**
- Revisit the Module 1 draft budget with updated numbers: post-credit-plan obligations, banking-aligned cash flow timing.
- This is the version that gets signed off by the coach as the Tier 1 completion artifact — not a new exercise, a refinement of existing work.
**Practical application:** Final budget worksheet (revision of Module 1's).
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Savings
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant sets a concrete savings target tied to their track's next goal.
**Key teaching points:**
- Emergency fund baseline first (even a modest 1-month-expense target), then a track-specific goal: security deposit/down payment (Housing) or startup capital (Entrepreneur).
- Automate what can be automated — a recurring transfer beats a manual one every time.
**Practical application:** Savings target worksheet, split into emergency fund + track-specific goal.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Financial Calendar
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant builds a recurring bill/deposit/review calendar.
**Key teaching points:**
- Combine income timing (Module 1), bill due dates (Cash Flow Management), and a monthly self-review date into one recurring calendar.
- A monthly review date is the habit this lesson is actually trying to install — the calendar itself is just the scaffold.
**Practical application:** Financial calendar template, populated with the participant's actual dates.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Financial Milestones
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant and coach define the specific milestones that get tracked in the case record and shown on the dashboard.
**Key teaching points:**
- These milestones become literal `Milestones` table records (see `docs/06-airtable-backend-schema.md`) — this lesson is where the participant and coach agree on what they'll be.
- Typical milestones: "Credit action plan implemented," "Utilization under target," "Emergency fund at $X," "Banking documentation ready."
- Completing this lesson + coach sign-off on the defined milestones is what triggers the Financial Stability Certificate and unlocks Tier 2 (per `docs/02-kajabi-curriculum.md`'s gate).
**Practical application:** Milestone-definition worksheet, reviewed live with coach.
**Downloads:** standard set; this lesson's output is entered directly into Airtable by the coach, not just kept as a PDF.
