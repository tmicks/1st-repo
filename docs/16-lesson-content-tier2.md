## Module 1 — Rent Ready

### Documentation

> Landlords ask for a fairly standard set of documents before they'll approve an application, and this lesson is about assembling that set once so you're not scrambling every time you apply.
>
> **The Standard Packet**
> - Photo ID
> - Proof of income — pay stubs, or the banking documentation you organized in Tier 1 Module 3
> - Rental history or references
> - Any program verification letter, if your pathway requires one
>
> Assemble it once and reuse it for every application — most rejections at this stage come from an incomplete packet, not a weak one. Keep both a digital copy (scanned or PDF) and a printed copy ready to go.

### Application Packet

> With your documents gathered, it's time to put together a complete, submission-ready application packet.
>
> Add a short cover letter or introduction to your documents — a few sentences explaining your current stability and your participation in this program. This small addition measurably improves response rates with landlords who are on the fence.
>
> If anything in your credit or rental history needs context, address it proactively in your cover note rather than letting a landlord discover it unexplained — landlords respond better to a direct explanation than to silence.
>
> Have your coach review the full packet before you submit it anywhere for the first time.

### Income Verification

> Every landlord wants to see proof you can afford the rent, but what counts as proof varies from one landlord to the next.
>
> **Common acceptable forms:**
> - Recent pay stubs (usually the last 2-3)
> - Bank statements
> - An employer letter
> - Benefit award letters
>
> If you're self-employed or do gig work, your Tier 1 banking deposit-consistency record plus your prior year's tax return or 1099s serve as your verification instead.
>
> **One number worth knowing:** most landlords want to see gross income at 2.5-3x the monthly rent. That ratio is what sets a realistic rent search range in your next lesson.

### Rental Budget

> Now take the ratio from Income Verification and apply it to your actual Tier 1 budget — not just your gross income, which ignores everything else you're already paying.
>
> Include the full cost of housing, not just rent: utilities, renter's insurance, and parking if it applies to you.
>
> Set a target range rather than a single number. A range keeps your search realistic without narrowing it so far that nothing fits.

## Module 2 — Housing Stability

### Maintaining Housing

> Getting housing and keeping it are two different skills, and this lesson is about the second one.
>
> On-time rent payment is the single highest-leverage thing you can do for both your current housing stability and your future rental history. If a problem comes up — a repair, a temporary income disruption — communicate with your landlord or property manager before it becomes a missed payment, not after.
>
> Know your rights and responsibilities as a tenant. *[Link to your state/local tenant rights resource here.]*

### Financial Planning

> Now that your housing costs are real numbers instead of estimates, go back to your Tier 1 budget and Financial Calendar and update them with your actual rent, utilities, and any other housing costs.
>
> Build a housing-specific buffer on top of your general emergency fund — something set aside specifically for move-in costs, a deposit, or a repair you're responsible for as a tenant.

### Lease Education

> Before you sign anything, you should be able to read and understand it.
>
> **Always check:**
> - Term length and renewal/notice requirements
> - Fees — late fees, pet fees, parking fees
> - Who's responsible for maintenance
> - Early termination terms
>
> **Red flags:** clauses that try to waive tenant rights that can't legally be waived, vague maintenance language, or fee structures that seem unreasonable.
>
> It's normal to ask a landlord to clarify or even modify a clause before you sign — that's standard practice, not confrontation.

## Module 3 — Homeownership

### Mortgage Basics

> Homeownership starts with understanding a few core terms so the rest of this module makes sense.
>
> **PITI** — principal, interest, taxes, and insurance — the four pieces of a typical mortgage payment. **Fixed vs. adjustable rate** — whether your interest rate stays the same or can change over time. **Loan term** — usually 15 or 30 years.
>
> **Loan types to know:** conventional, FHA, VA (if you're a veteran), and any first-time-homebuyer or workforce-specific programs available in your area — ask your coach what's available locally.
>
> **Pre-qualification vs. pre-approval:** pre-qualification is a quick estimate; pre-approval is a lender's actual commitment based on verified documents, and it's what sellers take seriously.

### Down Payment Planning

> Most people assume they need 20% down — for many first-time and workforce-specific loan programs, it's actually 3-3.5%. This lesson sets a realistic target instead of an assumed one.
>
> Ask your coach about down payment assistance programs available locally or through partner organizations — for many participants, this is the single biggest lever in making homeownership possible sooner rather than later.
>
> This target connects directly to the savings goal you set back in Tier 1 Module 4 — here, it gets refined into a specific dollar amount and timeline.

### Closing Costs

> Closing costs are the expense that catches first-time buyers off guard — this lesson exists so it doesn't catch you.
>
> Budget 2-5% of your loan amount for costs like the appraisal, inspection, title fees, and origination fees, plus prepaid escrow.
>
> Ask about seller concessions and closing cost assistance programs — both can reduce how much cash you need upfront, and it's always worth asking rather than assuming they're unavailable.

### Homeownership Pathway

> This lesson brings the whole module together into one sequenced plan: your mortgage type, your down payment target and timeline, and your closing cost budget, all in one place.
>
> It also flags what's still missing — credit, savings, or documentation — and routes you back to the relevant Tier 1 or Tier 2 content, or to a conversation with your coach.
>
> Whether you're pursuing homeownership right away or choosing to stay in stable rental housing for now, your coach's sign-off on this pathway plan is what completes Tier 2 and earns you your **Housing Access Certificate**.
