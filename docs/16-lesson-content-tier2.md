## Module 1 — Rent Ready

### Documentation
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant assembles the standard document set landlords require.
**Key teaching points:**
- Standard packet: photo ID, proof of income (pay stubs or the banking documentation from Tier 1 Module 3), rental history/references, and any program verification letter if applicable.
- Assemble once, reuse for every application — most rejections at this stage are incomplete packets, not weak applications.
- Keep both digital (scanned/PDF) and printed copies ready.
**Practical application:** Document-gathering checklist mapped to the standard packet above.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Application Packet
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant assembles a complete, submission-ready rental application packet.
**Key teaching points:**
- Combine Documentation lesson output with a cover letter/introduction — a short note explaining current stability and program participation measurably improves response rates with landlords who are on the fence.
- Address credit/derogatory items proactively in the cover note if relevant, rather than letting a landlord discover them unexplained.
- Have the packet coach-reviewed before first submission.
**Practical application:** Application packet template + cover letter template.
**Downloads:** standard set; Example = a filled sample packet.

### Income Verification
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands what counts as acceptable income verification and prepares it.
**Key teaching points:**
- Acceptable forms vary by landlord: pay stubs (typically most recent 2-3), bank statements, an employer letter, or benefit award letters.
- Self-employed/gig participants: use the banking deposit-consistency record from Tier 1 plus tax documentation (prior year return or 1099s) as the verification substitute.
- Ratio to know: most landlords want gross income at 2.5-3x monthly rent — this sets realistic rent-search range, covered next lesson.
**Practical application:** Income verification worksheet, tailored to employment type (W-2 vs. self-employed/gig).
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Rental Budget
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant sets a realistic rent range based on their Tier 1 budget.
**Key teaching points:**
- Apply the income ratio from the prior lesson against actual budget numbers from Tier 1 Module 4 — not against gross income alone, since that ignores existing obligations.
- Include full housing cost, not just rent: utilities, renter's insurance, parking if applicable.
- Set a target range (not a single number) to keep the search realistic without over-constraining it.
**Practical application:** Rental budget worksheet, pulling directly from the Tier 1 final budget.
**Downloads:** standard set.

## Module 2 — Housing Stability

### Maintaining Housing
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands the ongoing behaviors that keep housing stable once secured.
**Key teaching points:**
- On-time rent payment is the single highest-leverage behavior for both current housing stability and future rental history.
- Communication with landlords/property managers when a problem arises — proactively, before a missed payment, changes outcomes significantly.
- Know your rights and responsibilities as a tenant (program should link to state/local tenant rights resources here).
**Practical application:** "If X happens, do Y" quick-reference checklist for common housing stability scenarios (repair needed, temporary income disruption, lease question).
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Financial Planning
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant integrates housing costs into their ongoing financial plan.
**Key teaching points:**
- Revisit the Tier 1 budget and Financial Calendar with actual housing costs now locked in.
- Build a housing-specific buffer (beyond the general emergency fund) for move-in costs, deposits, or unexpected repairs the tenant is responsible for.
**Practical application:** Updated budget/calendar worksheet reflecting confirmed housing costs.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Lease Education
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant can read and understand a standard lease before signing.
**Key teaching points:**
- Key clauses to always check: term length, renewal/notice requirements, fees (late, pet, parking), maintenance responsibilities, early termination terms.
- Red flags: illegal clauses (waiving tenant rights that can't legally be waived), unusually vague maintenance language, unreasonable fee structures.
- When and how to ask a landlord for clarification or a modification before signing — normalize this as standard practice, not confrontation.
**Practical application:** Lease review checklist, applied to the participant's actual lease before signing.
**Downloads:** standard set.

## Module 3 — Homeownership

### Mortgage Basics
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands core mortgage concepts and loan types.
**Key teaching points:**
- Core terms: principal, interest, taxes, insurance (PITI), fixed vs. adjustable rate, loan term length.
- Loan type overview: conventional, FHA, VA (if serving veterans), and any first-time-homebuyer or workforce-specific programs available in the service area.
- Pre-qualification vs. pre-approval — the difference matters and affects how seriously an offer is taken.
**Practical application:** None yet — conceptual foundation for the next 3 lessons.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Down Payment Planning
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant builds a savings plan toward a specific down payment target.
**Key teaching points:**
- Typical ranges by loan type (3-3.5% for many first-time programs up to 20% conventional) — helps set a realistic target rather than assuming 20% is required.
- Down payment assistance programs: research and list what's available locally/through partner organizations — often the single biggest lever for this population.
- Connects directly to the Tier 1 Module 4 savings goal — this lesson refines that goal into a specific dollar target and timeline.
**Practical application:** Down payment savings plan worksheet with target amount and timeline.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Closing Costs
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands and budgets for closing costs beyond the down payment.
**Key teaching points:**
- Typical range (2-5% of loan amount) and what's included: appraisal, inspection, title, origination fees, prepaid escrow.
- Seller concessions and closing cost assistance programs as ways to reduce upfront cash need — worth asking about, not assuming unavailable.
- This is commonly the line item that surprises first-time buyers — the goal of this lesson is that it doesn't.
**Practical application:** Closing cost estimate worksheet.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Homeownership Pathway
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant has a complete, sequenced plan from current state to closing.
**Key teaching points:**
- Sequences everything from this module: mortgage type selection → down payment target/timeline → closing cost budget → realistic house-hunting timeline.
- Identifies remaining gaps (credit, savings, documentation) and routes back to relevant Tier 1/2 content or coach follow-up as needed.
- Coach sign-off on this pathway plan is what completes Tier 2 and triggers the Housing Access Certificate (per `docs/02-kajabi-curriculum.md`'s gate) — whether or not the participant is pursuing homeownership immediately or has chosen to stay in stable rental housing for now.
**Practical application:** Full homeownership pathway plan template, coach-reviewed.
**Downloads:** standard set; this lesson's output is the Tier 2 completion artifact.
