## Module 1 — Business Formation

### Choosing an Entity
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands the common entity types and picks the right one for their situation.
**Key teaching points:**
- Sole proprietorship vs. LLC vs. S-corp/corp — liability protection, tax treatment, and complexity tradeoffs at a plain-language level, not a legal opinion.
- For most first-time business owners in this program, an LLC is the common default — this lesson explains why, and when it isn't (e.g., specific licensing/contractor requirements that dictate a different structure).
- Directs participants to confirm with a qualified professional (attorney/CPA) for their specific situation — this program educates, it doesn't replace legal/tax advice.
**Practical application:** Entity comparison worksheet, weighing the participant's specific situation against each type.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### LLC
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands the actual steps to form an LLC in their state.
**Key teaching points:**
- Standard steps: choose/reserve a name, file articles of organization with the state, pay the filing fee, obtain any required licenses/permits for the specific business type.
- State-specific: filing fee and processing time vary — link to the state's actual Secretary of State filing portal here.
- Registered agent requirement — what it is and options for meeting it.
**Practical application:** State-specific LLC filing checklist.
**Downloads:** standard set; Template = filing checklist tailored to your service state(s).

### EIN
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant obtains an Employer Identification Number.
**Key teaching points:**
- What it's for: opening a business bank account, filing business taxes, hiring employees — required for the Business Banking module regardless of whether the participant plans to hire.
- How to get one: free, direct through IRS.gov — flag clearly that paid "EIN filing services" are unnecessary; this is a 15-minute free process.
- Common mistake to avoid: applying before the LLC filing is confirmed, which can create a mismatch.
**Practical application:** EIN application walkthrough checklist.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Operating Agreement
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands why an operating agreement matters, even for a single-member LLC.
**Key teaching points:**
- What it covers: ownership percentages, decision-making authority, what happens if a member leaves or the business dissolves.
- Even single-member LLCs benefit — it reinforces the liability separation between owner and business that makes the LLC protection meaningful in practice.
- Not filed with the state, but should be kept with business records — banks and future capital partners may request it.
**Practical application:** Operating agreement template, participant fills in their specific terms.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Business Checklist
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant confirms every formation step is complete before moving to Business Banking.
**Key teaching points:**
- Consolidates Modules 1 lessons into one master checklist: entity chosen, LLC filed and confirmed, EIN obtained, operating agreement drafted, any required licenses/permits secured.
- Coach reviews this checklist before Module 2 unlocks — this is the Module 1 gate.
**Practical application:** Master formation checklist, coach sign-off.
**Downloads:** standard set; this lesson's output is the Module 1 completion artifact.

## Module 2 — Business Banking

### Business Accounts
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant opens a dedicated business bank account.
**Key teaching points:**
- Why separation matters: commingling personal/business funds undermines the liability protection the LLC exists to provide, and makes bookkeeping and lending applications far harder later.
- What's needed to open one: EIN, formation documents, operating agreement, personal ID — this lesson is where all of Module 1's outputs get used.
- Consider the program's banking partner model (same PNC-model concept referenced in Tier 1 Module 3) for business accounts if the partnership extends to business banking.
**Practical application:** Business account opening checklist.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Recordkeeping
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant sets up a basic, sustainable recordkeeping system.
**Key teaching points:**
- Minimum viable system: track every income and expense with a receipt/invoice trail, whether via spreadsheet or basic accounting software.
- Separate categories that matter for tax time from day one, rather than reconstructing them later.
- Consistency beats sophistication — a simple system used weekly outperforms an elaborate one used quarterly.
**Practical application:** Recordkeeping template (spreadsheet-based starter system).
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Accounting Basics
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands basic accounting concepts needed to read their own numbers.
**Key teaching points:**
- Revenue vs. profit vs. cash on hand — the three numbers new business owners most often confuse.
- Basic income statement structure: revenue minus expenses equals profit, read monthly not just annually.
- When to bring in a bookkeeper/accountant vs. continue self-managing — a practical threshold (e.g., revenue level, transaction volume, or time cost) rather than "eventually."
**Practical application:** Simple income statement worksheet, populated from Recordkeeping lesson data.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Cash Flow
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant manages business cash flow distinctly from personal cash flow (Tier 1).
**Key teaching points:**
- Business cash flow timing is often more irregular than personal income — invoice/payment lag, seasonal demand, upfront material costs.
- Building a cash reserve sized to the business's specific volatility, not a generic personal-finance emergency fund number.
- Common failure mode: profitable on paper but cash-poor due to timing — this lesson exists specifically to prevent that trap.
**Practical application:** Business cash flow projection worksheet (simple 90-day view).
**Downloads:** standard set.

## Module 3 — Business Capital Pathways

### Understanding Business Credit
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands how business credit differs from personal credit.
**Key teaching points:**
- Business credit is tracked separately (Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, Equifax Business) and, once established, protects personal credit from business risk.
- Building it requires the business entity and EIN (Module 1) plus the business bank account (Module 2) to already be in place — this module assumes those are done.
- Early steps: net-30 vendor accounts (next lesson), a business credit card used and paid consistently, on-time payment to any business trade accounts.
**Practical application:** None yet — foundational lesson for the rest of the module.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Vendor Accounts
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant establishes net-30 vendor tradelines to begin building business credit.
**Key teaching points:**
- What a net-30 vendor account is and how it reports to build a payment history distinct from the owner's personal credit.
- How to find vendors that report to business credit bureaus and are relevant to the participant's actual business (not vendor accounts for their own sake).
- Payment discipline here matters as much as it did in Tier 1's personal credit work — the mechanics differ, the behavior doesn't.
**Practical application:** Vendor account research checklist tailored to the participant's industry.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Financing Basics
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands the landscape of small business financing options.
**Key teaching points:**
- Overview: SBA loans, microloans (particularly relevant for newer/smaller businesses), business lines of credit, and — for clean energy contractors — financing tied to project pipelines (connects forward to Module 4).
- What lenders actually evaluate: business credit, personal credit (still relevant for newer businesses), cash flow history, time in business, collateral.
- Avoid predatory financing (high-cost merchant cash advances, etc.) — this lesson names specific red flags rather than a vague warning.
**Practical application:** Financing options comparison worksheet.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Responsible Capital Management
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands how to use capital, once accessed, without jeopardizing the business.
**Key teaching points:**
- Match financing term/type to its use — short-term operating gaps shouldn't be financed with long-term debt structures, and vice versa.
- Debt service coverage concept in plain terms: don't take on payment obligations the business's actual cash flow (Module 2) can't support.
- What over-leveraging looks like in practice and how to course-correct early.
**Practical application:** Capital-use decision worksheet ("what is this money for, and can cash flow support the payback?").
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Capital Planning
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant builds a capital plan tied to specific, sequenced business goals.
**Key teaching points:**
- Consolidates Module 3: what capital is needed, for what specific purpose, from what likely source, on what timeline.
- Coach reviews this plan before Module 4 unlocks (for participants pursuing the EEP → EEC / clean energy path) or before Program Completion evaluation (for participants whose track ends here).
**Practical application:** Capital plan template, coach-reviewed.
**Downloads:** standard set; this lesson's output is the Module 3 completion artifact.

## Module 4 — Illinois Shines

### Overview
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands what Illinois Shines is and how it connects to their business goals.
**Key teaching points:**
- Illinois Shines is the state's solar renewable energy credit (SREC) program — explain in plain terms what it funds and why equity participation matters within it.
- How this module fits the broader Entrepreneur Pathway: it's a specific capital/project pipeline available to contractors in the clean energy space, layered on top of the general business formation and capital work already completed in Modules 1-3.
- Sets up the EEP and EEC definitions covered in the next two lessons.
**Practical application:** None yet — program orientation lesson.
**Downloads:** standard set; PDF should reflect current, accurate Illinois Shines program details (verify against the program's official materials before publishing, since incentive program terms change).

### EEP
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands what an Equity Eligible Person (EEP) is and whether they qualify.
**Key teaching points:**
- Definition and qualifying criteria as defined by the Illinois Shines program (verify current criteria against official program documentation before publishing).
- Why EEP status matters: it's the pathway toward the EEC status covered next, and toward specific training/apprenticeship opportunities within the clean energy contractor pipeline.
- How EEP status is documented/verified for program and funder reporting purposes (ties to `docs/06-airtable-backend-schema.md`'s Outcome Reporting "EEP→EEC Advancement" tracking).
**Practical application:** EEP eligibility self-check worksheet.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### EEC
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands what an Equity Eligible Contractor (EEC) is and the path from EEP to EEC.
**Key teaching points:**
- Definition and requirements to become an EEC (again, verify current criteria against official Illinois Shines documentation).
- What EEC status unlocks: contractor participation in the Illinois Shines project pipeline (next lesson) and eligibility for the EEC Incubator (Module 5).
- This is the specific, named outcome the "EEP → EEC Support" metric in `docs/00-overview.md` is built to track.
**Practical application:** EEP-to-EEC pathway worksheet — gap analysis between current EEP status and EEC requirements.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Contractor Participation
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands what it looks like to actually operate as a contractor within Illinois Shines.
**Key teaching points:**
- How projects get assigned/bid within the program structure.
- Compliance and reporting requirements contractors are expected to meet.
- Business-operations implications: this connects back directly to Modules 2-3 (business banking, recordkeeping, cash flow, capital) — a participant who skipped ahead without those foundations will struggle here.
**Practical application:** Contractor readiness checklist.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Project Pipeline
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands how to access and evaluate available projects.
**Key teaching points:**
- Where/how projects in the pipeline become visible to eligible contractors.
- How to evaluate project fit against current business capacity (crew size, equipment, cash flow runway) rather than taking on more than the business can deliver.
- This lesson is the practical bridge into Module 5's Incubator, for participants whose capacity isn't yet sufficient to take on pipeline projects independently.
**Practical application:** Project evaluation worksheet.
**Downloads:** standard set.

## Module 5 — EEC Incubator

### What is an Incubator
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant understands the purpose and structure of the EEC Incubator.
**Key teaching points:**
- An incubator provides structured support (mentorship, capacity-building, project readiness coaching) for EECs not yet ready to operate fully independently in the project pipeline.
- Who it's for: contractors who've completed EEC status but assessed (Project Pipeline lesson) that their capacity isn't yet sufficient for independent project participation.
- Sets expectations for time commitment and what "graduating" the incubator looks like.
**Practical application:** None yet — orientation lesson for the module.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Business Capacity
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant assesses and builds their actual operating capacity.
**Key teaching points:**
- Capacity dimensions: crew size/skill, equipment, bonding/insurance capacity, cash flow runway to cover a project before payment.
- Honest self-assessment against real project requirements from the pipeline, not aspirational capacity.
- Building a capacity-growth plan with concrete, sequenced steps rather than a vague "grow the business" goal.
**Practical application:** Business capacity assessment worksheet.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Mentorship
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant is matched with and engages a mentor through the Incubator.
**Key teaching points:**
- How mentor matching works and what a mentor relationship in this program actually involves (cadence, scope, what mentors can and can't do).
- What to bring to mentor sessions: specific, current business questions rather than open-ended check-ins.
**Practical application:** Mentor-session prep worksheet (goals for each session).
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Project Readiness
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant reaches a documented state of readiness to take on independent pipeline projects.
**Key teaching points:**
- Consolidates capacity assessment and mentorship progress into a readiness determination.
- Readiness isn't binary/all-or-nothing — this lesson may result in a partial-readiness plan (e.g., ready for smaller projects, not yet for larger ones) rather than a pass/fail gate.
**Practical application:** Project readiness assessment, coach/mentor reviewed.
**Downloads:** standard set.

### Scaling
**OBJECTIVE:** Participant has a plan to scale their contracting business beyond incubator support.
**Key teaching points:**
- What scaling actually requires next: additional capital (connects back to Module 3), additional crew/capacity (connects back to this module's Business Capacity lesson), and continued pipeline project experience.
- This is the final lesson of Tier 3 for EEP → EEC track participants — completion here, alongside the rest of Module 1-5, triggers coach sign-off for the Entrepreneur & Economic Participation Certificate.
- For participants who complete all three Tier certificates, this is also the point where the Economic Stability Certificate (Program Completion) is issued, and Phase 2 academy options (`docs/10-phase2-roadmap.md`) are introduced.
**Practical application:** Business scaling plan template, coach-reviewed — this is the Tier 3 (and potentially full-program) completion artifact.
**Downloads:** standard set.
