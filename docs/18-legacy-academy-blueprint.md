A **separate, new Kajabi Product line** from the Economic Stability Service (docs 00-17) — different branding, different structure (3 months, 3 steps, plus an optional specialty track), built for a different offer. The client's journey compresses to three words: **Think. Establish. Build.** At completion, the client doesn't say "I took a financial course" — they say "I built my Legacy Blueprint." Marketing message: **"Three steps to build your legacy."**

## Module 1 — STEP 1: THINK

### Financial Literacy Fundamentals

> Before we ask you to change anything about your financial position, we want you to understand it — completely, and without judgment. That's what this first lesson is about.
>
> **How Money Actually Works**
> Money is simply a tool for exchanging value — what you earn, what you owe, and what you keep are the three numbers that describe your financial life at any moment.
>
> **Income vs. Expenses**
> Income is everything coming in; expenses are everything going out. The gap between them — not the size of either one alone — is what determines whether you're building toward something or falling behind.
>
> **Needs vs. Wants**
> A need keeps you fed, housed, and functioning. A want makes life better but isn't required. Neither is bad — the goal is simply knowing which is which before you spend.
>
> **Saving, Debt & Interest**
> Saving is paying your future self first. Debt is borrowing from your future self. Interest is the price of that borrowing — and it works in your favor when you're the one earning it, and against you when you're the one paying it.
>
> **Financial Goals & Decision-Making**
> Every financial decision is really a small vote for the future you're building. This program exists to help those votes add up to something specific — not just "better," but a plan you can name.

### Banking Foundations

> A bank account is more than a place to hold money — it's the foundation every other financial goal in this program builds on top of.
>
> **Checking vs. Savings**
> A checking account is for money you'll spend soon; a savings account is for money you're setting aside. Keeping them separate — even informally — makes it much easier to know what's actually available to spend.
>
> **Direct Deposit**
> Setting up direct deposit is one of the simplest, highest-leverage moves in this entire program — it builds the consistent deposit pattern that lenders and landlords look for later, and it usually gets you paid faster than a paper check.
>
> **Banking Relationships & Fees**
> A bank you actually use consistently — not just one you opened once — is what builds a real banking relationship. Know your fee structure: monthly maintenance fees, overdraft fees, minimum balance requirements — and ask your bank directly if anything is unclear.
>
> **Automated Savings**
> Set up an automatic transfer to your savings account on payday, even a small one. Automating it means it happens whether or not you remember — and remembering is the part that usually fails.
>
> **Organizing Your Accounts**
> By the end of this lesson, you should be able to say exactly how many accounts you have, what each one is for, and how to access each one.

### Budgeting & Cash Flow

> A budget isn't a restriction — it's a plan for where every dollar goes before you spend it.
>
> **Building Your Monthly Budget**
> Start with your real income and your real expenses — not estimates. List every obligation, then everything discretionary, and make sure every dollar of income is assigned somewhere.
>
> **Cash-Flow Management**
> Money problems are often timing problems, not income problems. Map your income dates against your bill due dates — seeing the whole month at once usually reveals exactly where the pressure points are.
>
> **Expense Tracking**
> Track your actual spending for at least 30 days. Most people are surprised by at least one category once they see the real numbers instead of their memory of them.
>
> **Financial Priorities**
> Not every goal can be first. Rank yours — what has to happen this month, what can wait, and what's aspirational — so your budget reflects real priorities instead of trying to do everything at once.
>
> **Emergency Savings**
> Even a small emergency fund changes how every other financial decision feels, because it removes the panic of a single unexpected expense derailing your whole plan. Start with a small, specific target — you'll build it further as you move through this program.

### Your Financial Assessment

> This lesson brings Step 1 together into one honest picture of where you stand right now.
>
> **Your Current Position**
> Using what you built in the last three lessons — your income, expenses, savings, and banking setup — write down where you actually stand today. No judgment, just facts.
>
> **Your Goals**
> What are you actually working toward? Be specific — "financial stability" is a direction, not a goal. A goal has a number and a timeframe attached to it.
>
> **Your Barriers**
> What's actually in the way? Naming the real barrier — a credit issue, an income gap, a lack of savings — is what makes Step 2 and Step 3 useful instead of generic.
>
> **Your Opportunities**
> What's already working in your favor? Every plan is stronger when it's built on real strengths, not just a list of problems to fix.
>
> **What You Walk Away With**
> Completing this lesson — along with the rest of Step 1 — earns you your **Financial Literacy Certificate**, plus your **Personal Financial Foundation**, **Monthly Budget**, **Banking Action Plan**, and **Financial Goals Plan**. These become the foundation Step 2 builds directly on top of.
