## Module 1: Banking Fundamentals

### Post 1.1 — Why Banking Matters

**Body copy:**

> If you're currently unbanked or underbanked, you're not alone — but it's costing you. Check-cashing fees, money orders, and prepaid card fees can add up to hundreds of dollars a year, and without a bank account it's nearly impossible to build credit, qualify for housing, or save safely.
>
> In this module, you'll learn how to open and manage a bank account, understand the difference between account types, and avoid the fees that trap so many people in a cycle of paying more for less.

**Download:** *Banking Costs Comparison Worksheet* (check-cashing vs. bank account, annualized)

---

### Post 1.2 — Choosing the Right Account

**Body copy:**

> Not all bank accounts are the same. This lesson covers:
>
> - **Checking vs. savings accounts** — what each is for
> - **Traditional banks vs. credit unions vs. online banks** — pros and cons of each
> - **Second-chance banking programs** — options if you've had banking trouble in the past (ChexSystems issues, prior overdrafts)
> - **What to look for:** no/low monthly fees, no minimum balance requirements, free ATM access, mobile deposit
>
> **Action item:** Use the *Account Comparison Checklist* to compare 2–3 options before you open an account.

**Download:** *Account Comparison Checklist*

---

### Post 1.3 — Avoiding Fees & Overdrafts

**Body copy:**

> Bank fees are one of the biggest silent drains on a household budget. This lesson covers how overdraft fees work, how to turn off overdraft "protection" (which often costs more than it protects), how to set up low-balance alerts, and how to read your account disclosures so there are no surprises.

**Download:** *Fee-Avoidance Quick Reference Card*

## Module 2: Credit Building & Repair

### Post 2.1 — How Credit Actually Works

**Body copy:**

> Your credit score affects far more than loan approval — it impacts your interest rates, insurance premiums, apartment applications, and even some job applications. This lesson breaks down:
>
> - The 5 factors that make up your credit score (payment history, amounts owed, length of history, new credit, credit mix) and how much each one matters
> - The difference between a credit report and a credit score
> - Why your score can vary between the three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion)

**Download:** *Credit Score Factors Infographic*

---

### Post 2.2 — Reading Your Credit Report

**Body copy:**

> You're entitled to a free credit report from each bureau every week at AnnualCreditReport.com — the only site authorized by federal law for this. In this lesson, you'll pull your own report and learn to identify:
>
> - Accounts in good standing vs. accounts in collections
> - Hard inquiries vs. soft inquiries
> - Errors or signs of identity theft
>
> **Action item:** Pull all three reports and bring them to your next coaching session.

**Download:** *Credit Report Reading Guide (annotated example)*

---

### Post 2.3 — Disputing Errors

**Body copy:**

> Credit report errors are common — and disputing them is free and within your rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act. This lesson walks through how to identify a disputable error, write a dispute letter, submit it online or by mail, and what happens next (bureaus have 30 days to respond).

**Download:** *Dispute Letter Template*

---

### Post 2.4 — Building Credit From Scratch or Rebuilding

**Body copy:**

> Whether you have no credit history or are recovering from past credit problems, this lesson covers proven tools:
>
> - **Secured credit cards** — how they work and how to graduate to unsecured
> - **Credit-builder loans** — how they work through community banks and credit unions
> - **Becoming an authorized user** — benefits and risks
> - **On-time payment habits** — why payment history is 35% of your score and the single highest-leverage thing you control

**Download:** *Credit-Building Tools Comparison Chart*

---

## Module 3: Budgeting & Money Management

### Post 3.1 — Building Your First Real Budget

**Body copy:**

> A budget isn't about restriction — it's about giving every dollar a job so your money moves toward your goals instead of disappearing. This lesson introduces the 50/30/20 framework (needs/wants/savings) and a simpler income-minus-expenses tracking method for irregular income.

**Download:** *Monthly Budget Template (spreadsheet + printable)*

---

### Post 3.2 — Managing Irregular or Variable Income

**Body copy:**

> If your income varies month to month — gig work, seasonal work, tips, multiple part-time jobs — standard budgeting advice often doesn't fit. This lesson covers how to budget off your lowest expected month, build a buffer, and handle high-income months without overspending.

**Download:** *Variable Income Budget Worksheet*

---

### Post 3.3 — Building an Emergency Fund

**Body copy:**

> An emergency fund is what stands between a flat tire and a financial crisis. This lesson sets a realistic first target (a $500–$1,000 starter fund), then a longer-term goal (3–6 months of expenses), and covers where to keep it (a separate savings account, not checking).

**Download:** *Emergency Fund Tracker*

---

### Post 3.4 — Managing and Paying Down Debt

**Body copy:**

> This lesson compares the two most effective debt payoff strategies — the **debt snowball** (smallest balance first, for motivation) and the **debt avalanche** (highest interest rate first, for math efficiency) — and helps you choose which fits your situation, plus how to deal with collections calls and what debts to prioritize.

**Download:** *Debt Payoff Planner*

---

**Quiz block:** 5 questions on budgeting frameworks, emergency funds, and debt payoff strategies. Pass threshold 80%. Unlocks Module 4.
