## Module 1: Rental Readiness

### Post 1.1 — What "Rental Ready" Really Means

**Body copy:**

> Landlords and property managers screen applicants on four things: income, credit, rental history, and background. This lesson walks through each factor so you know exactly where you stand before you apply — and what's fixable before you start looking.

**Download:** *Rental Readiness Self-Assessment*

---

### Post 1.2 — Building Your Rental Application Packet

**Body copy:**

> A strong application packet gets you to the top of the pile, especially in competitive markets. This lesson covers what to include: photo ID, proof of income (pay stubs, offer letter, or benefit award letter), references (past landlords, employer), and a short cover letter introducing yourself.

**Download:** *Rental Application Packet Checklist*

---

### Post 1.3 — Understanding Leases & Tenant Rights

**Body copy:**

> Before you sign anything, you need to know what you're agreeing to. This lesson covers how to read a lease (term length, rent increases, fees, subletting rules), your rights as a tenant under [state] law, security deposit rules, and red flags that signal a lease you should not sign as-is.

**Download:** *Lease Review Checklist*

---

### Post 1.4 — Overcoming Common Barriers

**Body copy:**

> Past evictions, thin credit, or a criminal record don't have to be dead ends. This lesson covers second-chance rental programs, using a guarantor or co-signer, offering a larger security deposit, and how to write an explanation letter that addresses a landlord's concerns directly.

**Download:** *Explanation Letter Template*

---

### Post 1.5 — Module 1 Check-In

**Quiz block:** 5 questions on application packets, lease terms, and tenant rights. Pass threshold 80%. Unlocks Module 2.

## Module 2: Housing Stability

### Post 2.1 — Budgeting for Housing Costs

**Body copy:**

> Rent is rarely the only housing cost. This lesson covers the true cost of housing — utilities, renters insurance, transportation from that location, and a target of keeping total housing costs at or below 30% of gross income — and how to plan when that's not yet realistic.

**Download:** *True Housing Cost Calculator*

---

### Post 2.2 — Avoiding Eviction & Knowing Your Rights

**Body copy:**

> If you fall behind on rent, what happens next depends heavily on knowing your rights and acting early. This lesson covers the eviction process and timeline, your right to notice and to contest, emergency rental assistance programs, and how to communicate with a landlord proactively when you're at risk of missing a payment.

**Download:** *If You Can't Pay Rent: Action Steps Guide*

---

### Post 2.3 — Utilities, Renters Insurance & Household Budgeting

**Body copy:**

> This lesson connects back to your Tier 1 budgeting skills, applied specifically to housing: setting up utility accounts and avoiding deposits where possible, understanding what renters insurance actually covers (and why it's often cheaper than people assume), and building a housing-specific line in your monthly budget.

**Download:** *Housing Budget Worksheet*

---

### Post 2.4 — Module 2 Check-In

**Quiz block:** 5 questions on housing cost ratios, eviction rights, and renters insurance. Pass threshold 80%. Unlocks Module 3.

## Module 3: Saving Toward Homeownership

### Post 3.1 — Is Homeownership Right for You (and When)?

**Body copy:**

> Homeownership is a tool, not a requirement — this lesson helps you honestly assess readiness across four dimensions: financial (savings, debt, income stability), credit (score needed for different loan types), lifestyle (how long you plan to stay in the area), and market (what's realistic in your local market right now).

**Download:** *Homeownership Readiness Self-Assessment*

---

### Post 3.2 — Down Payment & Closing Cost Savings Strategies

**Body copy:**

> This lesson covers realistic savings targets, down payment assistance programs (state/local/first-time-buyer programs, employer programs), and closing cost ranges so you're not caught off guard by costs beyond the down payment.

**Download:** *Down Payment Savings Tracker*

---

### Post 3.3 — Credit for Mortgage Readiness

**Body copy:**

> Mortgage lenders look at credit differently than landlords. This lesson covers the credit score tiers that affect mortgage rates, how to prepare your credit specifically for a mortgage application in the 6–12 months before applying, and how debt-to-income ratio factors into approval.

**Download:** *Mortgage Credit Prep Checklist*

---

### Post 3.4 — Understanding Mortgages & Loan Programs

**Body copy:**

> This lesson introduces the loan types most first-time and moderate-income buyers use — FHA, USDA, VA (if applicable), and conventional loans with low down payment options — along with pre-qualification vs. pre-approval, and where to find a HUD-approved housing counselor for free 1:1 guidance.

**Download:** *Loan Program Comparison Chart*

---

### Post 3.5 — Module 3 Check-In

**Quiz block:** 5 questions on down payment assistance, mortgage credit readiness, and loan types. Pass threshold 80%. Unlocks Module 4.
