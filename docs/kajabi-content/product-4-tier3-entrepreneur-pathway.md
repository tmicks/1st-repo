## Module 1: Business Foundations

### Post 1.1 — Is Entrepreneurship Right for You Right Now?

**Body copy:**

> Starting a business can build wealth — but it can also strain finances if launched before the foundation is solid. This lesson is an honest gut-check: your financial runway, the problem your business idea solves, who'll actually pay for it, and whether this is a side-income idea, a full replacement-income goal, or something in between.

**Download:** *Entrepreneur Readiness Self-Assessment*

---

### Post 1.2 — From Idea to Business Concept

**Body copy:**

> This lesson turns a rough idea into a testable concept using a simple one-page business model canvas: what you offer, who it's for, how you'll reach them, how you'll get paid, and what it costs you to deliver.

**Download:** *One-Page Business Model Canvas*

---

### Post 1.3 — Choosing a Business Structure

**Body copy:**

> This lesson compares sole proprietorship, LLC, and (briefly) S-corp — liability protection, tax treatment, cost and complexity to set up — and gives a decision tree for which structure fits a business at the stage you're at now, with room to change later.

**Download:** *Business Structure Decision Tree*

---

### Post 1.4 — Registering & Licensing Your Business

**Body copy:**

> This lesson walks through the practical registration steps: choosing and registering a business name (DBA if needed), getting an EIN from the IRS (free, takes minutes), state/local business licenses and permits, and where to check what your specific business type and city require.

**Download:** *Business Registration Checklist*

## Module 2: Money Management for Your Business

### Post 2.1 — Separating Business & Personal Finances

**Body copy:**

> Mixing personal and business money is one of the most common early mistakes — it makes taxes harder, hides whether the business is actually profitable, and can undermine the liability protection of an LLC. This lesson covers opening a business bank account, and a simple system for tracking income and expenses from day one.

**Download:** *Business Banking Setup Checklist*

---

### Post 2.2 — Pricing & Basic Bookkeeping

**Body copy:**

> This lesson covers how to price a product or service to actually cover costs and pay yourself, the difference between revenue and profit, and a simple bookkeeping habit (weekly, not annually) using free/low-cost tools appropriate for a new business.

**Download:** *Pricing Calculator + Simple Bookkeeping Template*

---

### Post 2.3 — Small Business Taxes 101

**Body copy:**

> This lesson demystifies self-employment tax, quarterly estimated payments, what's deductible as a business expense, and why setting aside a percentage of every payment received (commonly 25–30%) for taxes prevents a painful surprise at filing time.

**Download:** *Tax Set-Aside & Deduction Tracker*

---

### Post 2.4 — Module 2 Check-In

**Quiz block:** 5 questions on business banking, pricing, and tax basics. Pass threshold 80%. Unlocks Module 3.

## Module 3: Building Business Credit & Accessing Capital

### Post 3.1 — How Business Credit Works

**Body copy:**

> Business credit is separate from personal credit and, done right, protects your personal credit while giving your business its own borrowing power. This lesson covers business credit bureaus (Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business, Equifax Business), getting a D-U-N-S number, and the timeline for building a business credit profile.

**Download:** *Business Credit Building Roadmap*

---

### Post 3.2 — Vendor Credit & Business Credit Cards

**Body copy:**

> This lesson covers net-30 vendor accounts as a common first step to establishing trade lines, how to use a business credit card responsibly to build history, and the difference between credit that reports to business bureaus vs. cards that only affect your personal credit.

**Download:** *Vendor Credit Starter List (Net-30 Accounts)*

---

### Post 3.3 — Funding Options for Early-Stage Businesses

**Body copy:**

> This lesson surveys realistic funding sources at this stage: microloans (including CDFI/community lender options), small business grants (and how to spot scams), friends-and-family funding done properly (with written terms), and why most early businesses should avoid high-interest merchant cash advances.

**Download:** *Funding Options Comparison Chart*

---

### Post 3.4 — Preparing to Apply for Capital

**Body copy:**

> Whether applying for a microloan or a grant, lenders and grant reviewers want the same core package. This lesson covers what to prepare in advance: a simple business plan summary, 3–6 months of bank statements, basic financial projections, and how to write a compelling narrative about your business.

**Download:** *Capital Application Prep Checklist*

---
